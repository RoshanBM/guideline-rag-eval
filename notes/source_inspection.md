  Reading the examples: hashes `6a23a277`, `169a766e`, `8ffb1680` are **fi**; `e71e9475`, `f0f57f4b` are **fl**; `a846d610`, `2dce5ce9` are a spacing acute accent, `3153d706`, `29b7749d` a grave and `a0fbcf41` a diaeresis, each *following* its base letter; `44d02e14` is a dotless ı used as the base of *í* (*Martí*, checked visually). Accented glyphs occur only in names (acknowledgements, references, annexes).

- **Bullets** are a symbol glyph extracted as `[`: 28 stand-alone `[` lines (bullet drawn as a separate span beside the text) plus 56 lines starting `[ `. They must be converted to a list marker by position, not by character, because `[` also occurs legitimately (e.g. *[website]*).
- **Line-end hyphens:** 29 body lines end in `letter-`. Spot checks show almost all are real compound hyphens split at the line end (`evidence-`, `cost-`, `Cost-`, `meta-`, `Attribution-NonCommercial-`, `peer-`, `angiotensin-`, `life-`), so the hyphen must be kept by default and only removed when the joined word is attested elsewhere in the document.
- **No font-style information:** span flags are {0: 3122, 4: 108}; Type3 fonts carry no bold/italic flags, so headings must be detected by size and colour (chapter 24.9 pt red, section 12 pt red, sub-heading 10 pt red, recommendation box title white) and grading lines by text pattern.
- **Broken lines in headings:** long chapter and section titles wrap (e.g. *2 Method for developing the / guideline*, *3.3 Cardiovascular disease risk assessment as guide to initiation of / antihypertensive medications*); consecutive heading-styled lines must be merged.
- **Figures are text, not images.** Figs 1–6 and Fig. A3.1 are vector drawings whose labels extract as loose lines in drawing order (e.g. Fig. 2's flow chart, Fig. 5/6's dose-titration boxes). Figs 5 and 6 contain the only drug- and dose-specific content in the guideline (telmisartan, amlodipine, hydrochlorothiazide, chlorthalidone doses), so they need layout-aware reading order rather than being dropped. Table 1 and the Annex 4 PICO tables are two-column layouts without ruling lines.
- Pages with no text at all: 2, 12, 61.

## 4. Recommendation: primary source for AWaRe

**Use the EPUB as the primary source for the AWaRe primary care chapters, and the PDF only as a
secondary source for page verification and (optionally) the infographic pages.**

Reasons, all measured above:

1. **Dosing tables are real tables in the EPUB.** Table 23.5 is a `<table>` with one `<tr>` per drug
   and one `<td>` per population, so adult/child/duration cells are unambiguous. pdfplumber silently
   drops half of the drug rows of the same table from the PDF (the unshaded ones), fuses footnote
   markers into words and splits the table at the page break. A dosing-accuracy evaluation cannot be
   built on silently missing rows.
2. **Structure is explicit.** One XHTML file per chapter, `h1`/`h2`/`h3` for chapter/section/subsection,
   `<caption>` for table titles and `<tfoot>` for table notes. In the PDF all of this would have to be
   inferred from font size, colour and position.
3. **AWaRe category survives.** Access/Watch/Reserve is a CSS class (`back_verde`, ...) in the EPUB; in
   the PDF it is only a background colour drawn behind the drug name.
4. **Clean Unicode text.** No ligature losses, PUA icons or running headers to strip.
5. **Citations are not lost.** `epub:type="pagebreak"` markers carry the printed page numbers, which
   match the PDF page labels, so chunks can still cite printed pages.

Caveats to handle:

- The one-page clinical **infographics are JPEG-only in the EPUB**. They mostly restate the chapter
  text and tables, but contain a few short summary statements not phrased identically elsewhere. Either
  skip them (simplest, no loss of dosing data, since every regimen is in the HTML tables) or extract
  those pages from the PDF with layout handling. Recommended: skip them initially and record this as a
  known gap.
- The EPUB (ISBN 978-92-4-006937-4) and PDF (ISBN 978-92-4-006238-2) are different editions of the same
  2022 book; their Table 23.5 content matches, but a spot check of each chapter's dosing table against the
  PDF is worth adding to the AWaRe parser's tests.
- Markup is hand-edited (e.g. `<colgroup>` with 2 columns for a 3-column table), so the parser should
  read cells from `<tr>/<td>`, not from `<colgroup>`.

### 5.4 Extraction problems

The text layer is clean Unicode: no Private Use Area code points; fonts HelveticaNeue (1239), StoneSans (235), HelveticaNeue-Bold (231), MyriadPro-Regular (200), MyriadPro-Bold (50), Wingdings3 (36), HelveticaNeue-Italic (20), MyriadPro-Semibold (12), HelveticaNeue-Medium (11), HelveticaNeue-CondensedB (11), MyriadPro-It (7), Calibri (2) (span counts). Real ligature characters (ﬁ, ﬀ) are expanded by `normalise_text`.

1. **Every bullet is present twice**: a drawn StoneSans glyph (200 spans) and a "•" in the text font (200 spans), so naive extraction gives "••". Sub-bullets are a StoneSans "o" plus a Wingdings3 decoration (36 spans), giving "oo".
2. **Line-break characters inside spans**: 18 spans (mostly table cells, e.g. "≥7.0 mmol/L\n") end in a line-break character, which would split table rows.
3. **Side boxes and narrow text beside images.** Risk-factor boxes sit beside body paragraphs on the same lines (e.g. p. 21 *Diabetic kidney disease is a* | *Risk factors for diabetic kidney disease*), so naive line extraction interleaves them. They are separable by colour (box text #58595B, body #231F20). In Annex 2 body text runs beside photographs and their bold grey captions (pp. 30–32, 34–35).
4. **Superscripts** (13 spans under 8 pt) are separate spans, sometimes wedged into the gap between two words (*human insulin*¹ *to oral*), and bullet glyphs are offset vertically, so lines must be grouped by vertical centre. A superscript *2* after *m* is an exponent (*1.73 m²*), not a footnote marker. The text layer keeps a space before one marker (*unsuitable ², a*).
5. **Flowcharts** (Figs 1, 2, Annex 1) are MyriadPro text inside rounded rectangles; extracted line by line, the parallel branches interleave (*REVIEW IN 3 MONTHS ... Urine ketones ≥2+ Urine ketones <2+*). The rectangles are vector drawings, so text can be assigned to its box; **the arrows are drawings only**, so the decision logic (which box leads where) is not in the text layer at all.
6. **Footers** (*N HEARTS – D Diagnosis and management of Type 2 diabetes*) are removed by position (y ≥ 790).

### 5.6 Internal inconsistencies (recorded as `source_discrepancy`, values not corrected)

- **gliclazide-unit**: Fig. 1 prints 'GLICLAZIDE 80 g 2 x daily'; every other gliclazide dose in the document is in mg (Fig. 1 itself: '160 mg gliclazide' as the twice-daily total, and 'gliclazide 80 mg 2 x daily'; Fig. 2: 'gliclazide 80 mg bid'). Kept as printed; not corrected.
- **screening-bmi**: Screening criterion for asymptomatic adults: Fig. 1 '40+ years old and BMI ≥25' versus Fig. 2 '40+ years old with BMI >25' (a BMI of exactly 25 qualifies only under Fig. 1).
- **hyperglycaemia-18**: Threshold for marked hyperglycaemia: '≥18 mmol/L' in the text (Diagnostic tests, p. 13; urgent referral criteria, p. 26) versus '>18 mmol/L' in Fig. 1 and Fig. 2 (Fig. 1's routine branch is 'FPG ≥7 mmol/L and <18 mmol/L').
- **hyperglycaemia-treatment**: Treatment for FPG/RPG >18 mmol/L (or symptomatic FPG ≥15 mmol/L) with urine ketones <2+: Fig. 1 gives metformin 1000 mg 2 x daily plus gliclazide ('80 g 2 x daily' as printed), whereas Fig. 2 begins gliclazide 80 mg bid only, without metformin.
- **glycaemic-targets**: Glycaemic targets use different comparison signs: HbA1c '7.0%' (p. 17), '≤7.0%' (p. 27) and '<7.0%' (Table 7); FPG '≤7.0 mmol/L' (pp. 17 and 27; Fig. 2 '≤7.0 mmol/l') versus '<7.0 mmol/L' (Table 7); postprandial PG '≤9.0' (pp. 17 and 27) versus '<9.0' (Table 7). A value of exactly 7.0 is on target under one wording and not under the other.
- **urine-ketones**: Urine ketone threshold for referral: '≥2+' (Fig. 1 'Urine ketones ≥2+'; Fig. 2 'if ketones ≥2+'), 'urine ketone 2+' (Fig. 2, severe hyperglycaemia) and '>2+' (urgent referral criteria, p. 26); a result of exactly 2+ is a referral under the first two only.
- **severe-hypoglycaemia**: Severe hypoglycaemia threshold: 'plasma glucose ≤2.8 mmol/L (50 mg/dL)' (p. 19) versus 'plasma glucose <50 mg/dl or 2.8 mmol/l' (Fig. 2).
- **figure-references**: Figure cross-references in Annex 2 do not match the captions: the monofilament text cites '(Fig. 6 and Fig. 7)', but Fig. 6 is 'Posterior tibial artery palpation' (Fig. 7 is 'Sites to be tested for loss of protective sensation', Fig. 8 the monofilament test); the tuning fork text cites '(Fig. 8)', but the tuning fork is Fig. 9. Kept as printed.
- **first-review-interval**: First review before starting metformin: Fig. 1 reviews after 1 month ('REVIEW IN 1 MONTH (or immediately if symptoms appear)', then 'IF goal not achieved BEGIN METFORMIN 500 mg daily'), whereas Fig. 2 reviews after 3 months ('REVIEW IN 3 MONTHS If goal not achieved BEGIN METFORMIN 500 mg once daily').
- **table3-footnote**: Table 3: the note '* Overnight fast of 8–14 hours.' has no '*' marker in the table body (the fasting row carries only '**'); it presumably refers to the fasting row, but this is not stated.
